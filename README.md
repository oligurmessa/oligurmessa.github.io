Holistic Signature Work: The Ethical Stewardship of Artificial Intelligence
Introduction: Technology in Service of the Common Good
As we stand in the midst of an unprecedented acceleration in artificial intelligence, the question facing computer scientists is no longer just "What can we build?" but rather, "What should we build, and how will it impact humanity?" Throughout my time at the University of St. Thomas, my education has been grounded in the university's mission to educate students to be morally responsible leaders who think critically, act wisely, and work skillfully to advance the common good. For my Signature Work, I have chosen to focus on the ethical stewardship of artificial intelligence and digital privacy.

This theme is deeply personal to me. As a student on the fast-track M.S. path focusing on machine learning research, I spend my days working with diffusion models, generative audio, and deep neural architectures. The immense power of these technologies offers incredible potential to solve complex problems, but they also pose significant risks to privacy, equity, and human agency if deployed recklessly. To truly advance the common good, technology must be developed with a profound respect for human dignity. This requires a deliberately interdisciplinary approach, looking at technological advancement not just through the lens of performance metrics, but through the lens of human impact.

An Interdisciplinary Approach: Computer Science + Philosophy
To properly address the ethical development of AI, I have learned to analyze my work through two distinct disciplinary lenses: Computer Science and Philosophy.

Computer Science provides the analytical framework to build, optimize, and scale complex systems. It teaches me how to reduce inference steps in diffusion models or minimize latency in generative audio pipelines. However, Philosophy—specifically ethical theory—provides the moral framework necessary to evaluate the consequences of those systems.

This interdisciplinary synthesis is best illustrated by my non-CS artifact, a research paper I authored for my upper-level philosophy seminar titled "The Ethical Dimensions of Artificial Intelligence." In this paper, I examined the societal impacts of multimodal vector search and open-weight Models-as-a-Service (MaaS) APIs through the lens of utilitarian and virtue ethics. I argued that developers have a moral imperative to prioritize user privacy over data monetization.

This wasn't just an academic exercise; it directly influenced my technical work. When I architected SoulSpect, a privacy-first personal intelligence app, I applied these philosophical principles to my code. Instead of relying on cloud-based processing that could compromise sensitive user data, I engineered the app for on-device inference using ChromaDB and RAG for semantic search. By combining my CS skills in backend architecture with my philosophical grounding in digital ethics, I created a product that respects the user's digital autonomy—a direct application of technology serving the common good.

Human-Centered Engineering and the Common Good
Developing professional and technical competencies is not a linear process; it requires engaging with real-world, human-centered challenges. Two of my portfolio projects, HallPals and GeezNet, perfectly demonstrate how my perspective on technology's role in society has evolved from purely technical problem-solving to human-centered engineering.

In the HallPals project, the situation was clear: Residence Assistants (RAs) needed a more efficient way to manage duty operations and incident reporting. The obstacle was that existing procedures were fragmented and slow, leading to frustrating shift handoffs. As the backend architect, my action was to engineer an AI-assisted platform with role-based access control and contextual incident guidance. The result was a highly successful pilot program with 10 RAs that yielded a 30-minute efficiency gain per shift. This project taught me that the best software doesn't just feature cutting-edge tech; it actively relieves human burden. My technical and soft skills—specifically cross-functional communication and user empathy—complemented each other to build a tool that fundamentally improved the daily lives of my peers.

Similarly, my research on GeezNet highlights how technical achievements can align with personal values. I implemented deep architectures (CNN, ResNet50, InceptionV3) for Ge'ez numeral recognition. Achieving 97.8% accuracy was a satisfying technical milestone, but the true value of the project was its contribution to the digitization and preservation of historical scripts. It demonstrated to me that machine learning can be a powerful tool for cultural preservation, ensuring that the historical artifacts of marginalized languages are not left behind in the digital age.

The Power of Failure: Iteration as a Professional Discipline
Growth mindset requires a willingness to confront and learn from failure. One of the most transformative challenges I faced occurred during my Drowsiness Detection course project.

The context of this project was ambitious: I set out to build a multi-layer neural network completely from scratch for eye-state classification, using only NumPy. I wanted to deeply understand the math behind the frameworks I normally use. However, the execution did not go as hoped. I attempted to write the forward pass, backward pass, ReLU activation, and Softmax gradient descent all in one monolithic block of code. Because of a hidden dimensionality bug in the backpropagation matrix multiplication, the model entirely failed to learn, and the gradient descent diverged into infinity.

This failure was deeply frustrating, but it was incredibly clarifying. It taught me the critical importance of modular testing and defensive programming. I learned that in computer science, you cannot build a reliable complex system on an unverified foundation. I changed my entire approach, rewriting the codebase to unit-test each layer's forward and backward pass individually against known PyTorch outputs before attempting integration. As a result, the final model successfully converged to 92% accuracy. More importantly, this "failure learning change" cycle instilled in me a disciplined, patient approach to debugging that I now carry into my high-level diffusion model research.

Beyond Graduation: A Commitment to Digital Equity
My time at St. Thomas has shown me that technical capability is only half of the equation; the other half is community engagement. I volunteered at Second Harvest Heartland, where I worked in a team to repackage bulk fresh fruits and vegetables into family-sized portions for distribution to local food shelves. The primary challenge was maintaining strict quality control and high-speed efficiency to ensure that thousands of pounds of perishable produce were sorted safely before spoiling. By collaborating effectively on the assembly line, my team directly addressed food insecurity in Minnesota, turning raw, bulk agriculture into immediate, dignified nutritional support for families in need. This experience reinforced my commitment to digital equity and structural resource distribution, proving how coordinated logistical efforts are vital to advancing the common good.

As I transition from my undergraduate studies into the fast-track M.S. program and eventually into the tech industry, my St. Thomas education has thoroughly prepared me to engage with the ethical stewardship of AI. I plan to continue developing both my technical and interpersonal skill sets by remaining engaged in research that prioritizes efficiency and accessibility, such as my current work optimizing AI audio for user-generated content.

Ultimately, I do not just want to be an engineer who pushes the boundaries of machine learning; I want to be a professional who ensures those boundaries are pushed safely, ethically, and equitably. The portfolio of stories, successes, and failures collected here serves as a foundation for that future. By continuously viewing computer science through a human-centric, interdisciplinary lens, I am ready to build thoughtful software that honors the dignity of the user and advances the common good.
